# Verification-chip methodology (public paragraph)

Every finding Nimbir shows carries one of four states, and the state is never carried by colour
alone: each chip shows a glyph, a word, and a shape. `✓ Verified` means the claim behind it was
checked against the primary source it cites — a hash-verified rule citation or a code page verified
against X12.org's own published list on a dated pass. `! Qualified` means the check ran and returned
an answer with a stated caveat — the check is only as strong as the caveat beside it. `✕
Contradicted` means the cited source actively disagrees with the claim (a rule whose effective date
falls after the claim it is being applied to). `○ Unchecked`, drawn with a dashed border, means
nobody has verified it yet, and it always offers a next step: spend one remaining check on it now,
or queue it for the next mission when this run's budget is gone. One rule protects all four words:
below the tier that can actually pay for primary-source verification, the chip is **withheld** —
never redefined. A chip that means something different at different tiers is a chip that means
nothing: the moment a cheaper-tier Verified and a verified Verified look identical while resting on
different evidence, the verdict becomes decoration. Withheld is honest; a softened chip is not. And
when a run's verification budget runs out, the work never stops — research and review continue,
verification stops, and the interface says so at the exact claim where it happened, inline, never
as a modal. The four verdict colours appear on claim chips and the budget meter and nowhere else.
